02

The problem

Interest wasn’t the issue. Readiness was.

Anyone could book.

The coach was relying on discovery calls to determine whether prospects were actually suitable for the programme.

So the calendar contained curious professionals, free-advice seekers, people without the financial capacity, people not ready to change — and the occasional genuinely qualified prospect.

The sales call had become the qualification mechanism.

Could we move some of that qualification earlier in the journey?

06

The strategy

Three principles shaped the build.

Don’t persuade everyone. Help the right people recognise themselves.

The page speaks deliberately to experienced professionals with substantial expertise — not anybody who feels vaguely dissatisfied at work.

Qualification begins before the form.

The copy establishes the programme’s depth, investment and expectations early enough to discourage poor-fit prospects without making the brand feel hostile.

Position the application as mutual evaluation.

“Let’s determine whether this is the right next move for you” reduces the status anxiety of applying while preserving a meaningful threshold.

That makes the call more diagnostic — and less corrective.

10

The copy lab

Three small choices with a large strategic footprint.

Good funnel copy often turns on decisions that look minor on the page but change how the buyer interprets the next step.

Copy decision 01
Apply→See if you’re a fit

Frame the action as mutual evaluation.

“Apply” can create unnecessary status anxiety. “See if you’re a fit” keeps the threshold while making the decision feel two-sided.

Copy decision 02

Address age directly.

Jay’s age is not merely demographic information. It is an objection. Ignoring it would leave a trust gap exactly where reassurance is most needed.

Copy decision 03

Sell the transition, not the coaching.

Jay does not wake up wanting coaching. He wants a credible route into his next professional chapter. That distinction changes the entire page.
